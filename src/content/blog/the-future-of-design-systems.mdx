## Systems should create direction

A design system works best when it gives a team a clear direction without trying to answer every possible product question. Tokens, components, and documentation establish the shared language. Product teams still need judgment to decide how that language should respond to a specific problem.

The useful work is not the number of components in the library. It is the quality of the decisions encoded inside them: spacing that establishes rhythm, typography that preserves hierarchy, and interaction rules that remain predictable across different surfaces.

## Constraints leave room for character

Strong systems make the common path obvious while leaving intentional room for expression. A campaign page and an account screen may share foundations without looking identical. That balance keeps the product coherent without making every experience feel interchangeable.

The future of design systems is less about accumulating components and more about maintaining a small, legible set of agreements that teams can trust.
